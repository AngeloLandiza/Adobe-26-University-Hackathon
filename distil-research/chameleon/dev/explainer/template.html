How it works

Six stages, each judged before it stayed

1 · today

Fixed floor plans

Today's engine tries a few fixed plans (a stack, a row, columns), fits the pieces in, and its grader picks the best-scoring one.

2 · bisection

Cut to the content

Cut the canvas along its long side, again and again, so each piece gets space by importance and by what it needs to stay readable.

3 · detail fixes

Fix what the judge named

Safer margins, a minimum logo size, legal text that fits in a few lines, no stranded last words, one alignment axis, decorations kept round and whole.

4 · hierarchy & near-fit

Keep the order of importance

The CTA, subhead and fine print stay clearly smaller than the headline. When the shape barely changes, a plain shrink of the original is kept.

5 · layers

Keep what sits on what

Backgrounds fill the new canvas, and text stays inside the card, band or circle it sat on, moving with it as one piece.

6 · anchored

Keep every piece in its place

Each piece keeps its spot in the original, top-left or centred or bottom edge, while the design grows to fill the new canvas. Text boxes get a safety margin so no line can re-wrap at scoring time.

Evidence

Each step had to beat the one before

Both versions of the engine resize the same ads. Claude, acting as a strict senior designer with the official judging criteria, compares the two results wherever they differ. Wins and losses are counted per ad and size; "p" is the chance a split this lopsided is luck.

Today vs the final pipeline, on 50 unseen real ads

70–17

100 resize tasks from 50 real ad templates (Crello) kept aside and never used for building, tuning or choosing anything. Every pair was judged in both image orders; a disagreement counted as unsure (13 were). The two orders agreed 93 times in 100. p < 0.001. Today's engine also broke a hard structural rule 13 times on these ads; the final pipeline never did.

Share of decided tasks the final pipeline won, by target shape; the tick marks 50%.

Ship-readiness, out of 5

Claude's 1–5 score on the tasks where the two differ. 2 means "clearly broken in at least one important way", 3 means "acceptable, needs a designer's fix". Better, still far from 5.

A second judge agrees

Claude Sonnet re-judged two of the key comparisons blind to the first verdicts: anchored vs layers 43–12 (first judge 46–11), today vs layers 68–21 (73–17). Where both judges picked a side, they agreed 84% and 96% of the time.

Experimental

Can a language model design it?

Claude laid out ads directly from the ad's JSON, and the engine only fitted the text and checked the hard rules. Then a small open model that could run offline was trained on Claude's layouts. All results are against the final pipeline, judged in both image orders.

The scoring container has no internet, so Claude itself can't be used at scoring time; only a small local model could. More training data took it from clearly worse (3–18) to about even (11–13). It doesn't beat the final pipeline yet, so it isn't part of it.

Limits

Better, not finished

  • Real ads still average about 2 out of 5. The gain is real and confirmed on ads nothing was tuned on, but the bar is still low.
  • On the default ad, the final pipeline is not better at every size. At LinkedIn size it builds the cleaner row layout and then picks a weaker one, by 0.921 to 0.917 on the engine's grader. Picking between good layouts is the next target.
  • Anchored layouts win often on other ads (try Summer Flavours, Sunset Sessions or Cucumber Mint) but never on the three official Solstice samples, where the grader still prefers bisection. A learned picker trained on Claude's rankings did not fix this: its gain turned out to be judging noise.
  • Near-fit keeps a plain shrink when the shape barely changes (Portrait from a square ad). Claude preferred that, but the brief asks to "rearrange the design, not simply shrink or stretch it", so the official judge may disagree.
  • The judges are Claude models applying the official criteria, not the official evaluator. The real-ad tests use Crello templates set in the two Solstice fonts.
  • Everything lives in the research copy of the engine, behind switches that are off by default.
